# ICP Scoring & Enrichment Scoping

## The two-priority system

Every lead gets scored once, in the ICP Score stage, into one of two tiers. The condition looks trivial. Getting the threshold right took a full measurement pass against the live dataset.

**Priority 1, full pipeline.** Has a usable contact (website, or an MCS email once MCS became a source) and a review count above the threshold. Gets review analysis, AI personalization, full contact enrichment, and routes into the highest-personalisation email templates.

**Priority 2, enrichment-only.** Everything else. Gets contact enrichment if a domain exists, but no review analysis and no Call Prep Card, because there isn't enough signal to make either worth the API cost. Still gets a full CRM row and stays eligible for the generic fallback template and phone outreach.

```javascript
var hasContact = !!lead.website || !!lead.mcsEmail;
var priority = (hasContact && lead.reviewCount >= 8) ? '1' : '2';
```

### Why 8, not 10

The original threshold was 10 reviews, carried over from the Maps-only era when a website was the only way to reach a lead by email. Once MCS entered the pipeline as a source, two things changed the reasoning.

First, the review-count distribution shifted. The 25th percentile across the full MCS-matched dataset is 6 reviews, median 17. A threshold of 10 was discarding leads that still carried enough review text for analysis to produce something useful.

Second, the website requirement stopped making sense. It only existed because the enrichment API needs a domain to find an email. MCS supplies an email directly for 100% of its records, so the dependency that justified requiring a website no longer applies to MCS-sourced leads.

Lowered to 8, and an MCS email accepted as an equal substitute for a website. Not lowered further, because below roughly 6 reviews complaint extraction from review text becomes noise rather than signal. 8 sits just above that floor rather than at the point of maximum coverage.

## What each tier actually receives

| Stage | Priority 1 | Priority 2 (with contact) | Priority 2 (no contact) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Review analysis (complaint + quote) | Yes | No | No |
| AI personalization (Call Prep Card) | Yes | No | No |
| Contact enrichment | Yes | Conditional, see below | No |
| CRM row | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Email-eligible | If a valid address resolves | If a valid address resolves | No |
| Phone-eligible | Always | Always | Always |

Priority 2 leads with no contact at all aren't dropped from the pipeline. They're written to the CRM for the Day-2 phone leg, which needs nothing but a phone number and works off basic company data rather than a Call Prep Card.

## Scoping the enrichment API call

Every lead with a website used to trigger a call to the Instantly Lead Finder API, regardless of whether MCS had already supplied an email. Once MCS became a source, that was straightforward waste: paying to call an enrichment API to find something already in hand.

Rescoped to a four-way branch:

| Segment | Call the enrichment API? | Email used |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1, MCS email is generic (`info@`) | **Yes.** A named contact converts better than a role inbox | Enrichment result if found, else the MCS generic address |
| Priority 1, MCS email is personal (`kate.matthews@…`) | No | MCS |
| Maps-sourced, no MCS email | **Yes.** This is the only route to an email at all for this segment | Enrichment result |
| Priority 2 with an MCS email | No | MCS |

Net effect: enrichment calls fell by roughly half to two-thirds, and the segment that previously had no email at all (20 to 30% of a Maps-only run) closed to near zero on the MCS side, because MCS supplies the email directly rather than depending on an enrichment API finding one.

When the enrichment API can't find a match, the fields are left blank rather than blocking the row. The lead still gets written to the CRM, still gets its phone number from the source data, and can still be called on Day 2. A failed enrichment lookup removes a lead from the email channel, not from the pipeline.

## First-name fallback

When enrichment isn't called or doesn't return a name, the first name gets derived from the local part of a personal email address: `kate.matthews@…` becomes "Kate." Generic prefixes (`info`, `sales`, `enquiries`, `admin`) are explicitly excluded from this derivation, so a role inbox never picks up a fake first name. This one fallback rule cuts how often the pipeline falls back to a generic greeting, at zero additional API cost, since it's just string parsing on data already present.

## Trade and Trade Scene: deterministic, not AI-generated

Both fields feed almost every email template, including the fallback one that has no other personalisation data. They're computed with pattern matching against a controlled vocabulary, not generated by an LLM. This was a deliberate call: the fallback template's entire personalisation budget rests on these two fields being right every time, and pattern matching against a known technology list is more reliable for that than a model call that might occasionally hallucinate a trade.

Once MCS entered the pipeline, its `technologies` field turned out to be a materially cleaner input than Google Maps' `categories` field for this. MCS uses a controlled vocabulary (`solar_pv`, `ashp`, `battery`), while Maps categories are free text and noisy ("Contractor," "Business to business service"). The matching logic now prefers MCS technologies where present and falls back to Maps categories only when a lead has no MCS record.

## Coverage, stated plainly

| Lead segment | Reachable by email | Reachable by phone |
|---|---|---|
| Priority 1, full data | If enrichment or MCS resolves an address | Always |
| Priority 2, has a contact | If enrichment or MCS resolves an address | Always |
| Priority 2, no contact | No | Always |

No segment of the CRM is unreachable by every channel. The phone number, sourced from either MCS or Google Maps, has close to universal coverage, which is why the Day-2 call is treated as a channel in its own right rather than a nice-to-have addition to email.
